# Redesigning Your Website? Protect the Search Traffic You Already Have

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

A new website is meant to bring more business. Too often, the first month after launch brings less. Enquiries from Google fall, nobody can say why, and the team starts to wonder whether the old site was better after all.

In most cases the design is not the problem. The problem is that pages which used to bring visitors have moved or disappeared, and search engines were never told where they went. The good news is that this is easy to prevent if you plan for it before launch.

## Before you change anything: make a list

Export every page on the current site that gets visits from search or has links from other websites. Google Search Console shows the pages and searches that bring visitors. Your analytics tool shows which pages lead to enquiries. Keep this list. It is the most valuable document in the whole project.

Mark the pages that bring enquiries. Some of them will look old or plain. Do not delete them because of how they look. Improve them and keep them.

## Map every old address to a new one

For each page on the list, decide where its visitors should land on the new site, and set up a permanent (301) redirect from the old address to that page. Send each one to the closest match: the old pricing page to the new pricing page, an old service page to the new service page.

Avoid the shortcut of redirecting everything to the home page. A visitor who clicked a link about a specific service and lands on the home page usually leaves.

Google's own guidance on site moves is clear on timing: keep the redirects for as long as possible, generally at least one year, so that search engines can transfer the old pages' signals to the new ones. From a visitor's point of view, keeping them indefinitely is even better.

## Watch the staging site

New sites are usually built on a hidden copy, and that copy is rightly blocked from search engines while it is being built. The most common launch mistake I see is that the block travels to the live site. A single setting can tell search engines to stay away from the whole new website.

On launch day, check that the live site is open to search engines, that the robots.txt file allows the pages you want found, and that no page you care about carries a "noindex" instruction.

## A launch-day checklist

- Test the redirects for your most important old addresses, one by one.
- Submit the new XML sitemap in Search Console.
- Confirm that analytics is recording visits and that every enquiry form actually sends.
- Update your own internal links to point straight to the new addresses.
- Check speed on a phone. Google's Core Web Vitals give useful targets: the main content should load within about 2.5 seconds, the page should respond to a tap in under 200 milliseconds, and the layout should not jump around while it loads.

## The first eight weeks

Expect some movement in search results while Google recrawls the site. What you want to see is a dip that recovers, not a slide that continues.

Each week, look at three things: pages reported as "not found" in Search Console, enquiries from search compared with the same weeks before launch, and the pages that lost the most visitors. Most problems trace back to a missing redirect or a page that lost its content, and both are quick to fix once you know about them.

A redesign is a good moment to improve a website. It should not be the moment you lose the visitors it took years to earn.
