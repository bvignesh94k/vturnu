# Hiring a Digital Marketing Agency? Seven Questions to Ask Before You Sign

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

Most small and mid-sized businesses hire a marketing agency once every few years. Because they do it so rarely, they get little practice at it. The pitch meetings start to sound alike: growth, rankings, leads, a dashboard with plenty of colour. Six months later, some owners realise they bought activity rather than results.

I run a digital marketing agency, so I have sat on the other side of many of these meetings. These are the questions I would ask if I were the buyer. Several of them come straight from Google's own advice on hiring someone to work on your search visibility.

## 1. What will you measure, and when will I see it?

Ask to see a sample monthly report before you sign. Look for the numbers your business runs on: qualified leads, conversion rate, the cost of winning a customer and, in time, revenue. Traffic, rankings and impressions are useful early signals. They are not the result you are paying for.

## 2. Can you guarantee first place on Google?

The right answer is no. In its guidance [Do you need an SEO?](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo), Google says that if someone guarantees their changes will give you first place in search results, you should find someone else. Treat guaranteed leads from the first month the same way.

## 3. Who will actually do the work?

The people in the pitch are not always the people on the account. Ask for the names and roles of the team, who you will speak to each week, and whether any of the work is passed to another company.

## 4. What access do you need, and who will own the accounts?

Google advises that if an agency offers a search audit, you should give it read-only access to Search Console at that stage, not the right to make changes. The wider principle matters more. Your website, domain, analytics and advertising accounts should be owned by your company. The agency should be added as a user, so that the history and the data stay with you if you part ways.

## 5. Will you explain every change before you make it?

Google suggests asking whether an agency will share all the changes it makes to your site and the reasons behind them. A good agency is happy to. You learn how your own marketing works, and you can spot a bad idea before it goes live.

## 6. What do you need from us?

Notice whether the agency asks about your customers, your competitors, your sales process and what makes you different. Google puts it plainly: if they are not interested in you and your business, find someone who is. Results also depend on your side: quick approvals, product knowledge and honest feedback from your sales team about the leads they receive.

## 7. How does the contract end?

Agree the notice period and the handover at the start, while everyone is getting along. On exit, you should receive every file, login and report, and the agency's access should be removed.

## Three warning signs

- **Price is the only argument.** One common mistake is choosing an agency based only on low pricing or guaranteed rankings. A cheap retainer that produces nothing is the most expensive option.
- **The report is all activity.** Posts published, keywords tracked, hours spent. None of these tell you whether the phone rang.
- **No hard questions.** An agency that agrees with everything in the first meeting will struggle to tell you an uncomfortable truth later.

## Make the choice on paper

Ask every agency on your shortlist the same seven questions and write down the answers. Comparing them side by side makes the differences much clearer than a set of slide decks. For a longer checklist, including what to put in the brief, see this guide to [choosing a digital marketing agency](https://vturnu.com/blog/how-to-choose-a-digital-marketing-agency/).

The best agency relationships I have seen feel less like a supplier contract and more like a partnership with clear numbers on both sides. The questions above are how you find one.
