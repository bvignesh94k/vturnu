# Your Google Ads May Be Reaching People Who Can Never Buy From You

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

A local business sets up Google Ads, chooses its city, sets a budget and waits for the phone to ring. Clicks arrive. Calls do not. When someone finally looks at the reports, some of the clicks came from people in another state, or another country, who could never have become customers.

This is one of the most common ways small advertisers lose money, and it usually comes down to a single setting most people never open.

## The setting behind the problem

When you choose a location in Google Ads, there is a second choice under "Location options". According to [Google's own help pages](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722038), the default and recommended option reaches people who are in, regularly in, or who have shown interest in your chosen locations.

That last part matters. "Shown interest" can include someone sitting in another city who searched for something connected with your area. For a hotel or a tourist attraction, that is exactly the right audience. For a plumber, a clinic or a local accountant, it often is not.

The alternative is "Presence": people who are in or regularly in your target locations. Google notes that most campaigns see fewer impressions after switching away from the default. For a business that can only serve customers in one place, fewer but closer impressions are usually the better deal.

## Five checks that stop the leak

1. **Open the location report.** In your campaign, look at where the clicks actually came from. If a noticeable share came from places you cannot serve, the setting is costing you money.
2. **Choose the option that matches how you sell.** If customers must come to you, or you go to them, "Presence" is often the better fit. If people plan a visit from far away, keep the default.
3. **Read the search terms report.** It shows the exact searches that triggered your ads. Add irrelevant ones as negative keywords: jobs, free, training, and the names of places you do not cover.
4. **Match the schedule to your phone.** If nobody answers calls after six in the evening, showing call ads at night pays for missed calls.
5. **Send clicks to a page about the service.** A visitor who searched for one service should land on a page about that service, with a phone number and a short form, not on the home page.

## Measure calls, not clicks

Clicks are easy to count and easy to buy. What you want are calls and enquiries from people who can become customers. Set up conversion tracking for calls and forms before you judge any campaign, and review cost per enquiry every month, not cost per click.

Small budgets leave no room for waste. A few minutes on these settings can free up money that was quietly going to people who were never going to call.
