# The 95-5 Rule: Most of Your Buyers Are Not Buying Today

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

Every marketer knows the pressure to show results this month. Leads, enquiries and sales are counted every week, and anything that does not produce them quickly is questioned. A simple idea from marketing science explains why that pressure, left unchecked, can damage a brand's future.

## What the rule says

The idea is called the 95-5 rule. It comes from Professor John Dawes of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, in a paper published in 2021 by the B2B Institute at LinkedIn. His point is that up to 95% of business buyers are not in the market for many goods and services at any one time.

The arithmetic is easy to follow. If companies change a supplier, such as a bank or a law firm, about once every five years, then only about 20% of them are buying in any one year. That is about 5% in any one quarter. The other 95% are not ignoring you. They simply have no need yet.

## Why it matters

If only a small share of buyers is in the market today, then most of the people who see your advertising cannot respond to it now, however good it is. That does not make the advertising wasted. It means advertising mostly works by building memories that buyers use later, when the need arises.

When that day comes, buyers do not start with a blank page. They start with the few names they already know. A brand that spoke only to people who were ready to buy will be missing from that list.

## What marketers can do

1. **Plan for two audiences.** Keep the campaigns that capture today's demand, such as search ads and enquiry pages. Alongside them, keep a steady programme that reaches future buyers.
2. **Reach widely within your category.** You cannot tell in advance which company will need you next year. Narrow targeting of "hot" prospects misses most of tomorrow's market.
3. **Be easy to remember.** Use the same name, colours, tone and message everywhere, for years. Memory is built by repetition, not by novelty.
4. **Link the brand to buying situations.** Tell people when to think of you: when a contract is ending, when a team is growing, when a system is failing.
5. **Measure both time frames.** Count leads for the short term. For the long term, watch whether more people search for your brand name each year.

## A word of balance

The rule is a guide, not a law. Dawes presents it as a way of thinking, and the exact figure changes with how often a category is bought. It also does not say that lead generation is unimportant. The 5% who are buying today pay this year's salaries.

The lesson is about proportion. A marketing plan judged only on this month's leads will slowly stop talking to next year's customers.
