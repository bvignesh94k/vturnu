# Bots Are Filling In Your Forms: Five Ways to Keep Junk Out of Your CX Data

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

CX teams make decisions from what customers tell them. Contact volumes shape staffing. The words people type into forms and chat windows shape routing, topic reports and, more and more, the AI tools that summarise and answer for agents.

The problem is that a growing share of those "customers" are not customers at all.

## Half the traffic is not human

Imperva's 2025 Bad Bot Report found that automated traffic overtook human activity in 2024, making up 51% of all web traffic. Malicious bots alone accounted for 37%, up from 32% the year before. The report links much of the rise to AI tools, which have made it easier for people with little technical skill to build bots that behave like real visitors.

Contact forms, callback requests and chat widgets are easy targets. They are public, they accept free text, and they often trigger a human response. Every fake submission costs a little agent time, and the data it leaves behind costs more.

## How junk distorts CX numbers

Spam is usually treated as an inbox nuisance. For a CX team it is a data quality problem, and it shows up in at least four places:

- **Volume and staffing.** Inflated contact counts make demand look higher than it is, so forecasts and staffing plans drift.
- **Service levels.** Agents spend time opening, reading and closing junk, which pushes real customers further down the queue.
- **Topic and sentiment reports.** Bot text full of links, keywords and nonsense ends up in the same pool as genuine complaints and questions.
- **AI models and summaries.** Any tool trained or tuned on past contacts learns from the junk too, unless someone removes it first.

Surveys suffer as well. Satisfaction invites sent to fake addresses never come back, which quietly lowers response rates and can skew who is represented in the results.

## Five fixes

### 1. Stop bots at the form, without punishing people

Start with checks that real customers never see. A hidden "honeypot" field that only bots fill in, a check on how quickly a form was completed, and invisible challenges such as Google reCAPTCHA v3 or Cloudflare Turnstile, which assess a visitor without asking them to solve a puzzle. Avoid making every customer prove they are human. Friction at the form costs genuine contacts.

### 2. Validate on the server, not only in the browser

Many bots skip the web page and post straight to the form handler, so checks that run only in the browser are easy to bypass. Validate email and phone formats on the server, reject throwaway email domains, and limit how many submissions a single source can make in a short time.

### 3. Tag suspected junk instead of deleting it

Deleting junk hides the size of the problem. Add a "suspected spam" disposition or tag in your CRM or contact platform, keep the record for audit, and exclude it from reporting. Over time this gives you a junk rate you can track, and a sample you can use to tune your filters.

### 4. Report qualified contacts, not just contacts

Agree on what a qualified contact means for each channel. For a sales enquiry it might be a real company and a real need. For a support request it might be a verified customer. Report both numbers side by side, total contacts and qualified contacts, so a rise in junk never looks like a rise in demand.

### 5. Keep junk out of your analytics and AI

Before contact data feeds text analytics, topic models, chatbot training or knowledge suggestions, filter out anything tagged as junk. It is a small step that protects every downstream report and model, and it is far cheaper than explaining later why an AI assistant learned to answer spam.

## Start this week

You do not need a new platform to find out how big the problem is. Take the last 200 form or chat submissions, label each one as genuine, unclear or junk, and work out the share. If junk is more than a few percent, pick two of the fixes above and measure again in a month.

The goal is not zero spam. It is CX data that reflects real customers, so that staffing, reporting and AI decisions rest on what people actually need.
