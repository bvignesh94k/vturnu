# Google's November Core Update Is Rolling Out. Here Is a Calm Plan for Your Website

**Subtitle:** What Google has confirmed, what to leave alone, and the one comparison to run once it finishes.

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

On 10 November 2026, Google started rolling out its third core update of the year. If your search traffic moves up or down over the next few weeks, this is the most likely reason.

Core updates make people nervous because Google says very little about them. The good news is that the right response is simple, and most of it is about not overreacting. Here is what is confirmed, what to avoid while the update rolls out, and what to check once it is done.

## What Google has confirmed

- **Start:** 10 November 2026, at 10:06 a.m. Pacific time, according to the [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc).
- **Scope:** all countries and all languages.
- **Duration:** up to two weeks, which points to a finish around 24 November.
- **Description:** Google Search Central called it "a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content".
- **New guidance:** none. Google did not publish a blog post or new advice with this update.

This is the third core update of 2026, after the March and May updates. If an article claims to know exactly which sites this one targets, treat that as a guess.

## What a core update is, and what it is not

In its [guide to core updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates), Google describes them as significant, broad changes to its search systems, made several times a year. It says these changes "don't target specific sites or individual web pages".

So a drop after a core update is not a penalty, and there is no switch to flip back. Google's own comparison is a list of favourite restaurants: a new, better place can push another one down the list without the older one doing anything wrong. Your page may be exactly as good as it was last month, while other pages are now judged more helpful for the same search.

## What not to do while it rolls out

Rankings often swing back and forth during a rollout. Decisions made in the middle of it are usually decisions made on noise. For the next two weeks:

- **Do not rewrite your best pages in a panic.** Wait until you know which pages actually moved.
- **Do not change URLs or launch a redesign.** You would be mixing two causes, and you would not know which one moved your traffic.
- **Do not judge on daily numbers.** One bad Tuesday tells you nothing.
- **Do not buy links or disavow links on a hunch.** Neither is a fix for a broad ranking change.

## What to do this week

You can use the waiting time well. Four small jobs make the analysis later much easier:

1. **Note the dates.** Add 10 November as an annotation in your analytics, and check the dashboard for the official end date.
2. **Save a baseline.** In Search Console, export clicks, impressions and positions for 3 to 9 November, the last full week before the update, by page and by query.
3. **Split brand from non-brand.** Searches for your company name rarely move with core updates. Non-brand searches are where the change will show.
4. **Check your tracking and forms.** Make sure a broken tag or a failed form is not quietly causing a "drop" that has nothing to do with Google.

## What to do once it finishes

Google recommends waiting at least a full week after a core update completes before analysing your site in Search Console, then comparing that week with a week before the rollout started. If the update ends around 24 November, that points to the first days of December.

When you compare, look at pages and queries, not just the total:

- Which pages lost clicks, and for which searches?
- Is the page still the most useful answer for the person searching, or has a competitor's page become clearer, fresher or more complete?
- Did any pages gain? They often show what Google now prefers for your topics.

Then improve the pages that matter most to your business: answer the question sooner, update outdated facts, add real examples from your own work, and make the next step obvious. Google's guide also notes that it makes smaller core updates it does not announce, so good changes can show up before the next big update. If traffic fell and you are not sure why, Google's [guide to debugging search traffic drops](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops) is a solid checklist.

## Keep the facts straight

Most of the confusion around core updates comes from guesses repeated as facts. We are keeping a plain-English tracker of this one, with Google's own sources and the dates as they are confirmed, on our [November 2026 core update page](https://vturnu.com/updates/november-core-update-2026/).

The short version: note the dates, save your baseline, change nothing big for two weeks, then compare like with like. Calm, boring analysis beats a panicked rewrite every time.
