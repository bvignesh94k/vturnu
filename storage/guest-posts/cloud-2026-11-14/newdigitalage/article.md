# B2B advertisers are optimising for the wrong moment

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

Ask how a B2B campaign is doing and the answer is often a cost per lead. It is a tidy number. It arrives quickly, it fits on a slide and the ad platforms are very good at making it smaller.

That is the problem. A platform that is told to find form fills will find the people most likely to fill in forms. They are not always the people most likely to buy. Students, job seekers, competitors, suppliers and bots all complete forms. Give an algorithm that goal and enough budget, and it will learn who they are.

The sale, meanwhile, happens weeks or months later, in a CRM the ad platform never sees. So the platform keeps optimising for the moment it can measure. The campaign report keeps improving while the sales team keeps complaining. Both are telling the truth.

## The fix already exists

None of this is news to the platforms. Each of the main ones offers a way to send later sales stages back.

Google Ads calls its version [enhanced conversions for leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15713840), which it describes as an upgraded form of offline conversion import that uses data such as the email address a prospect typed into the form. Meta and LinkedIn each have a Conversions API that can receive events from a CRM. The idea is the same in every case: when a lead becomes qualified, or becomes a customer, the platform is told, and it can start looking for more people like that one.

In my view this feature is badly under-used in B2B advertising, and the reason is rarely technical. It is organisational. Sending sales data back needs marketing, sales and whoever owns the CRM to agree on three things.

## Three decisions before any setup

**What counts as qualified.** Write the definition in one sentence that sales would sign: the type of company, the type of need and the next step agreed. If the definition is vague, the signal will be too.

**Which stage to send.** The final sale is the signal that matters in the end. But in a long B2B cycle there are too few sales, arriving too late, for an algorithm to learn from. A qualified lead or a booked meeting gives more volume. Choose the latest stage that still happens often enough, and send the sale as well when it comes.

**Who keeps the data clean.** The feedback only works if stages are updated in the CRM promptly and consistently. That is a sales habit, not an adtech feature, and it is where I would expect a project to stall.

## What changes when you do it

Expect the cost per lead to go up. This is the moment to hold your nerve. The platform is now looking for a smaller, more valuable group, and that group costs more to reach. The number to watch is cost per qualified lead and, after a few months, cost per customer.

Expect the creative brief to change too. Advertising aimed at form fills rewards broad promises and gated downloads. Advertising aimed at qualified buyers rewards being specific about who the product is for, and who it is not for.

And expect privacy questions, rightly. Sending customer data to an ad platform needs a lawful basis, and your privacy notice and consent choices must cover it. Involve whoever is responsible for data protection before the first upload, not after.

## The honest report

A B2B campaign report should show three lines: leads, qualified leads and customers, with the cost of each. The first line will always flatter. The other two are the ones the business runs on.

Until the platforms are told what a good lead is, they will go on finding more of whatever fills in the form.
