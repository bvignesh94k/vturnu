# HubPages: kit for one article that you write yourself

## Why this is a kit, not a finished article
HubPages does not allow articles written by AI. Its staff say AI may be used for article ideas, outlines and SEO planning, but not for the article itself. They run detection checks and have banned accounts for publishing AI-written text. A ban would also tie your real name to a spam warning on a site that ranks well in Google.

So this kit gives you everything HubPages allows help with: the topic, title options, the outline, facts checked against Google's own help pages, the link plan and the photo plan. You write each section in your own words. Short sentences are fine. Your own examples make it stronger.

## Topic
A step-by-step guide to setting up a Google Business Profile so that local customers find the business and call. It suits HubPages readers (small business owners), it stays useful for years, and it matches your work in local search.

## Outline with notes
Each section has the points to cover and the facts with their sources. Write the sentences yourself.

### 1. Opening
- Who this is for: shops, clinics, workshops, salons, trades and any business that serves customers face to face, including businesses that visit customers at home.
- The problem: people search for a service "near me" or on Google Maps, and the businesses with complete, active profiles get the calls.
- Add one thing you have seen yourself in your work (keep it general, no client names), for example the most common mistake you notice on profiles.

### 2. Check that your business is eligible
- To qualify, a business must have in-person contact with customers during its stated hours.
- Not eligible: online-only businesses, rental or for-sale properties, lead generation companies, and virtual offices (a rented address where the business does not actually operate).
- Businesses that serve customers at the customer's location can still have a profile (as a service-area business).
- Source: Google, Business eligibility and ownership guidelines: https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036

### 3. Claim or create the profile, then verify it
- Search for the business on Google first; it may already have a profile that you can claim.
- Google asks you to verify the business before the profile goes fully live and before you can change key details such as the name. The method depends on the business, so tell readers to follow the steps Google shows them.
- Source: Google, Add or claim your Business Profile: https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778

### 4. Use your real business name, exactly as customers see it
- The name must match the real-world name used on the signboard, website and stationery.
- Do not add taglines, keywords, locations, phone numbers, website addresses or service details to the name. Google says unnecessary information in the name can lead to suspension.
- Example to give: "Sri Lakshmi Dental Clinic" is right; "Sri Lakshmi Dental Clinic Best Dentist in Chennai" is not. (Use your own example; keep it invented and clearly an example.)
- Source: Google, Guidelines for representing your business on Google: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177

### 5. Choose the right categories
- Pick the fewest categories that describe the core business, and make them specific.
- Google says the categories you choose affect your local ranking.
- Categories are not keywords: do not use them to describe features or to copy what nearby businesses use.
- Source: Google, Manage your business category: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669

### 6. Write the description
- Up to 750 characters.
- Say what you offer, what makes you different and how long you have been in business.
- Leave out website addresses, HTML and promotional prices.
- Tip for readers: write it the way you would explain the business to a new customer on the phone.
- Source: Google, Edit your Business Profile: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617

### 7. Hours, phone number, website and service areas
- Keep hours correct, including holiday hours. A customer who arrives to a closed door rarely comes back.
- Use a phone number that is answered during the stated hours.
- Link to the most useful page on your website for a new customer, which is not always the home page.
- (Your own advice; no source needed.)

### 8. Photos
- Real photos: the front of the building (so people can find it), the inside, the team at work, and finished work.
- Add new photos from time to time.
- (Your own advice; no source needed.)

### 9. Reviews: ask the right way
- Ask every customer, not only the happy ones.
- Never offer anything in return for a review: no discounts, gifts or payments. Google prohibits this, and it also prohibits discouraging negative reviews and selectively asking only satisfied customers.
- Google can remove such reviews and restrict the profile.
- Reply to reviews, good and bad, politely and briefly. Thank people by name; for a complaint, apologise and offer to sort it out offline.
- Sources: Google, Prohibited and restricted content: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114 and Tips to get more reviews: https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122

### 10. How Google decides which businesses to show
- Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence.
- Relevance: how well the profile matches the search (complete details help).
- Distance: how far the business is from the person searching.
- Prominence: how well known the business is, including reviews and how often it is mentioned around the web.
- Google also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
- Source: Google, Tips to improve your local ranking on Google: https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091

### 11. A 15-minute monthly routine
Give readers a short checklist:
- Check hours, phone and website are still right.
- Reply to any new reviews.
- Add two or three new photos.
- Post an update or offer if the business has news.
- Look at the profile's performance figures: calls, direction requests and website clicks.

- One or two lines that sum up: a complete, honest profile and a steady flow of genuine reviews do more than any trick.
