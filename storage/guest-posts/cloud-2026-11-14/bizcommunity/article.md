# Marketing does not end at the enquiry form

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

Most marketing reports stop at the same place. They count the visits, the clicks and the enquiries, and then they hand over to sales. What happens to the enquiry after that is treated as someone else's problem.

I think this is a weak point in most marketing plans. A business can spend months earning a visit from the right buyer, and then lose that buyer in an afternoon because nobody replied.

## The research is old, and it still stings

In 2011, Harvard Business Review published [The Short Life of Online Sales Leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) by James Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran and David Elkington. The researchers audited 2,241 companies in the United States, measuring how long each one took to respond to a test enquiry sent through the web.

The results were not flattering. Among the companies that replied within 30 days, the average response time was 42 hours. Nearly a quarter, 23%, never replied at all.

The same researchers also measured what speed is worth. Companies that tried to contact a potential customer within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a meaningful conversation with a decision-maker as those that waited even one hour longer.

That study is 15 years old, and I see no reason to think buyers have become more patient since. A buyer who fills in your form may well have filled in a competitor's form too. The first useful answer sets the standard for the rest.

## Why this is a marketing problem

It is tempting to say that slow replies are a sales issue. I do not agree, for three reasons.

First, marketing paid for the enquiry. Every campaign, every article and every advert exists to produce that moment. If the reply comes two days later, the money is spent and the result is lost. Extra traffic does not fix that.

Second, marketing owns the form. The form decides where the enquiry goes, who is told about it and what the buyer sees next. A form that sends everything to a shared inbox with no owner is a marketing decision, even if nobody made it on purpose.

Third, marketing is judged on quality. I believe marketing should be measured by qualified leads, conversion rates, customer acquisition cost and revenue, not by traffic alone. An enquiry that is never answered can never become a qualified lead, however good it was.

## Four things I would put in place

None of this needs new software. It needs a few decisions.

**Give every enquiry one owner.** Not a team, and not an inbox. One named person for each working day, with a backup. Shared responsibility is where enquiries go to wait.

**Set a reply target and write it down.** One working hour is a fair target for most businesses during office hours. It will not always be met. But a target that is written down gets measured, and one that is not gets forgotten.

**Tell the buyer what happens next.** The thank-you page and the confirmation email should say when a reply will come and who it will come from. If the office is closed, say so, and say when it opens. In my view, an honest waiting time is better than silence.

**Make the first reply useful.** An automatic "we have received your message" is not a reply. The first message from a person should answer at least part of the question, or ask the one thing needed to answer it.

## Put it on the report

The last step is the one that makes the others stick. Add two lines to the monthly marketing report: the time to first reply, and the share of enquiries answered within the target.

I would report the median, not the average, because one enquiry forgotten over a long weekend can distort an average. I would also show the slowest reply of the month. It is uncomfortable, and that is the point.

Once those numbers sit beside traffic and cost per lead, the conversation changes. Marketing and sales stop arguing about whether the leads were good, and start asking whether they were answered. It also makes the rest of the report easier to trust, because you know every enquiry had a fair chance. I have written separately about [how to measure qualified leads stage by stage](https://vturnu.com/blog/measure-qualified-leads-from-organic-search/), and response time belongs at the first of those stages.

A marketing plan that ends at the form is only half a plan. The enquiry is not the result. The conversation is.
