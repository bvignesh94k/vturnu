# What AI Assistants Tell Your Customers Before They Call You

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

**Summary:** Many customers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Google's AI summaries for a support number or a policy before they contact you. Whatever that answer says, your agents inherit it. Five checks help contact center leaders make sure those answers are right, and cut the avoidable contacts that wrong answers create.

A growing number of customers no longer start with your website. They type a question into ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Google, and they read the AI-written answer at the top. What is the support number for my bank? Can I return this after 30 days? How do I cancel my plan?

Whatever that answer says, your agents inherit it. If it gives an old phone number, a retired policy or the wrong channel, the call starts with a correction. Sometimes it starts with an upset customer who believes they were promised something.

This is not a marketing problem that happens somewhere else. It shows up in your queue, your handle time and your repeat contacts. Here is why it matters, and five checks any contact center team can run.

## Self-service already struggles to find the right answer

Gartner surveyed 5,728 customers in 2024 and found that only 14% of customer service issues were fully resolved in self-service. Even issues that customers described as very simple were fully resolved only 36% of the time. The most common reason was content: in 43% of cases, customers could not find material relevant to their problem.

AI assistants draw on the same public content. If your help pages are thin, out of date or hard to reach, the assistant has little to work with. It may fill the gap with someone else's page.

## The risk is not only a wrong answer

In August 2025, The Washington Post reported the case of a traveller who searched Google for a cruise line's customer service number. The number shown in the AI summary connected him to a scammer, who asked for his card details. Google said it had taken action on several fake numbers. WIRED reported that fraudsters appear to be posting fake numbers on lesser-known websites next to the names of real companies.

For a contact center, this is a direct threat. A customer who reaches a fake agent is a fraud case, a complaint and a trust problem at the same time. The best defence is to make your official contact details so clear and so consistent that there is no gap for anyone else to fill.

## Five checks to run this quarter

### 1. Ask the assistants your top contact reasons

Take your top ten contact drivers from your disposition codes or speech analytics. Turn each one into the question a customer would type, such as "How do I change my delivery address with [your brand]?" Ask it in ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Google, and note what each answer says. Mark each one as correct, outdated, wrong or missing. Ask the same questions every month, so you can see whether things improve.

### 2. Publish one clear contact page in plain text

Put every official phone number, email address, chat option and opening hours on one page, and say what each channel handles. Write the numbers as text, not inside an image. Then use exactly the same details everywhere: your website, your app store listings, your social profiles and your Google Business Profile. When the same facts appear in many places, an assistant is less likely to pick up a stray one.

### 3. Make sure your help center can be read

An assistant can only use what it can reach. Check three things with your web team:

- Help articles open without a login. Answers behind a login are invisible to every search engine and AI assistant.
- The answer is in the page itself, not loaded only after a script runs or a widget is opened.
- Your robots.txt file does not block the crawlers that feed these tools.

On the last point, the rules are published. Google's documentation says a page has to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet before it can appear as a source in AI Overviews or AI Mode. OpenAI's documentation lists OAI-SearchBot as the crawler that collects pages for ChatGPT search, separate from GPTBot, which is used for training. Blocking one does not block the other, so it is worth checking which ones your settings allow.

### 4. Retire old policies properly

Old refund rules, discontinued plans and past promotions often stay online long after they change. Assistants can find them and repeat them. When a policy changes, update the original page, show the date it changed, and remove or redirect the old copies, including PDFs.

### 5. Capture the "AI told me" moments

Add a simple disposition code or tag for contacts where the customer quotes an AI answer or a search result that turned out to be wrong. Review these each month with your knowledge management and web teams. Each one points to a page that needs fixing. Over time, this tag becomes the most direct measure you have of whether the public answers about your company are getting better.

## Who should own this

In most companies, the contact center owns the knowledge base, while the web or marketing team owns the website and its technical settings. AI answers sit between the two. A short monthly review with both teams, using the question list from check 1 and the tagged contacts from check 5, is usually enough to keep it under control.

## The point

Your knowledge base now has two audiences: your customers, and the AI assistants that answer on your behalf. Treat the second audience like a new channel. Test what it says, give it clear and current facts, and listen when your customers tell you it got something wrong.
