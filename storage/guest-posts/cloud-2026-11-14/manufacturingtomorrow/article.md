# Your RFQ Form Is the Front Door of the Factory: Seven Fixes That Bring Better Inquiries

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

A buyer has a drawing, a quantity and a deadline. They find three suppliers that look capable and send each one a request for quote. What happens next depends less on your machines than on a single web page: the RFQ form.

It is easy to put years of investment into the shop floor and an afternoon into that form. It often asks for too little to quote and too much to bother with. The result will be familiar to many estimators: half-complete requests, long email chains to collect the missing details, and good buyers who gave up before they pressed Send.

Here are seven fixes. None of them needs new software.

## 1. Ask for what estimating needs, and nothing else

Sit down with the person who prepares quotes and list what they need to price a job: the drawing or model, material, quantity now and per year, tolerances and finish, and the delivery date and location. Those are your fields. Anything else, such as a fax number or a compulsory "how did you hear about us", is a reason to leave.

## 2. Accept the files buyers actually have

Engineers work in CAD, so let them upload what they have: STEP, IGES, DXF and PDF at a minimum, and more than one file at a time. State the size limit on the page, and show a clear confirmation when the upload has worked. A form that silently drops a large file loses the job without anyone knowing.

## 3. Say what happens to their drawings

A drawing is intellectual property. Before a buyer uploads it to a company they have never dealt with, they want to know who will see it. Put two plain sentences next to the upload button: files are used only for quoting, and a non-disclosure agreement is available on request. It is a small detail that removes a real hesitation.

## 4. Put your limits next to the form

List the materials you work with, the part sizes you can handle, the tolerances you hold, your certifications such as ISO 9001, AS9100 or IATF 16949, and your minimum order. Some buyers will read that and realize the job is not for you. That is a good outcome. A request you cannot quote costs both sides time.

## 5. Promise a response time, and keep it

"We will reply within one business day" is a sentence that earns its place on the page, if it is true. Follow it with an automatic confirmation that repeats what the buyer sent, gives a reference number and names the person who will reply. The buyer now has something to forward to a manager, and a reason to wait for you before chasing the next supplier.

## 6. Send each request to the right desk

A new quote, a repeat order and a support question are three different jobs. Let the buyer choose which one it is, and route each to the right inbox. Make it a shared inbox with a named backup, not one person's email. Requests should not wait because somebody is on vacation.

## 7. Make it work from a phone and from every process page

Requests do not all start at a desk. Check that the form works on a phone, including the file upload. Then add a "Request a quote" button to each process and capability page that opens the form with that process already selected. Once a month, submit a test request yourself and time the reply.

## Four numbers worth tracking

Once the form is fixed, measure it:

- How many requests arrive each month
- What share can be quoted without asking the buyer for more information
- How long the first reply takes
- How many quotes are won

The second number is the one the form controls. If most requests need a follow-up email before they can be priced, the form is still asking the wrong questions.

The form is only the last step. Buyers have to find the right page first, and they search in several different ways: by specification, by application, by standard and by problem. I have set out [how industrial buyers search and which page answers each search](https://vturnu.com/blog/industrial-buyer-search-intent/) in a separate guide.

A buyer who sends you a drawing is trusting you with their design and their deadline. The page that receives it should show the same care as the parts you ship.
