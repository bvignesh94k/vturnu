# Five Checks That Help a Hotel's Own Website Win the Direct Booking

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

**Subhead:** Guests often compare a hotel's own website with its listing on a booking platform before they book. Five checks help make sure the hotel's own site gives them every reason to book direct.

Many travelers find a hotel on an online travel agency (OTA) and then search for the hotel by name before booking. They want to see the property in more detail, check whether a better rate or package is available, or confirm details before paying.

That second visit is an opportunity to win a direct booking. It is also where a booking can be lost. An OTA listing is usually complete, consistent and easy to use on a phone. If the hotel's own site is slower, missing details or harder to book, the guest returns to the platform and books there.

The following five checks help a hotel's own website match or improve on its OTA listing.

## 1. Show the total price, including mandatory fees

Price is one of the first things a guest compares, and it is now also a compliance issue.

The Federal Trade Commission's Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees (16 CFR Part 464) took effect on May 12, 2025. It covers short-term lodging, including hotels, motels and inns. Under the rule, it is unlawful to offer, display or advertise a price for a room without clearly, conspicuously and prominently disclosing the total price, including mandatory fees such as resort, amenity or destination fees. Fees must not be misrepresented. Certain charges, such as government taxes, may be left out of the total price, but they still need to be disclosed before the guest pays.

Hotels should test their booking flow from the first rate shown to the final payment page. The total price the guest first sees should match the total they are asked to pay, apart from charges the rule allows to be shown separately. Hotels using a brand or vendor booking engine should confirm how it displays fees.

## 2. Match the OTA listing on room details

Guests notice when the hotel's site says less than the OTA listing does. Each room type on the hotel's website should show at least what the platform shows:

- Bed type and maximum occupancy
- Room size, if it is published elsewhere
- Accessibility features, with photos where possible
- Amenities, including parking, breakfast and Wi-Fi terms
- Current photos of the actual room, not only the best room in the building

The details should match. If the platform says parking costs $15 a night and the hotel's site says it is free, the guest will trust neither.

## 3. Make the cancellation policy easy to find and read

Cancellation terms affect whether a guest books direct. A policy hidden in fine print, or written differently from the OTA's version, creates doubt at the moment of payment.

The policy should appear on the rate itself, before the guest enters payment details, in plain language: until what date and time a booking can be canceled free of charge, and what happens after that. Using the same wording on every channel avoids disputes later.

## 4. Give the guest a reason to book direct

A guest who has already found the same room at the same price on a platform needs a clear reason to book on the hotel's site instead. The reason does not have to be a lower rate. Common options include:

- Flexible cancellation not offered on other channels
- Breakfast, parking or early check-in included
- Room upgrades for direct bookings, subject to availability
- Loyalty points for brand members

Whatever the benefit is, it should be stated near the booking button, not on a separate page. Hotels that are part of a brand should check franchise agreements and brand standards before creating their own direct booking offers.

## 5. Make it easy to ask a question

Some guests want to ask a question before they book: about a late arrival, a group of rooms, an event or an accessibility need. If the only option is a generic contact form, many will book elsewhere.

The hotel's phone number should be visible on every page, especially on mobile, where it should be possible to tap to call. If the hotel offers email or chat, the website should say when someone will reply. Group, wedding and corporate inquiries deserve their own short form, asking for dates, number of rooms and any event space needed, so the sales team can reply with a quote quickly.

## Measuring the result

These checks are only useful if the hotel can see their effect. Two numbers are worth tracking each month:

- **Direct share of room revenue:** the share of room revenue booked through the hotel's own website and phone, compared with platforms.
- **Booking engine conversion:** the share of visitors to the booking engine who complete a booking. Most booking engines and web analytics tools can report this.

A small rise in direct share can cut commission costs and give the hotel a direct relationship with the guest for the next stay. The work starts with making sure the hotel's own website answers the guest's questions at least as well as the platforms do.
