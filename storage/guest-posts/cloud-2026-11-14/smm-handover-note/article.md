# The Handover Note: What Marketing Should Pass to Sales With Every Lead

**By B. Vignesh, Founder & CEO, VTurnU**

A rep opens a new lead in the CRM. It shows a name, an email address, a company and one word in the source field: "Website". The rep calls and opens with the only question available: "So, tell me a bit about your business."

On the other end, the buyer may have spent weeks on research. They read the pricing page twice, compared three suppliers and wrote a careful question in the form. Now they are asked to start again from the beginning.

Most of what the rep needed was already known somewhere in the company: in the form, in the website analytics, in the chat log and in the automatic emails. Nobody put it in one place. A short handover note fixes that.

## Six lines that should travel with every lead

The note should fit on a phone screen and take less than a minute to read. Six lines are enough.

**1. Their words.** The question or request exactly as the buyer wrote it, not a summary. "Do you offer a plan for teams under 20, and can it be set up before March?" tells a rep far more than "pricing inquiry".

**2. How they found you.** The source and, where you know it, the campaign, the search topic or the person who referred them. A referral from an existing customer calls for a different opening than a click on an ad.

**3. What they looked at.** The pages or documents they viewed before getting in touch: pricing, a particular service, a case study, a comparison page. Use only data you collected under your privacy notice, with any consent the law requires where you operate. If you do not have it, leave the line blank rather than guess.

**4. What they have already been told.** Every automatic reply, every chat answer and every price range or timeline already mentioned. If someone promised "a call tomorrow morning", the rep needs to know before tomorrow afternoon.

**5. Why they qualify.** The facts that matched your lead definition: company, role, size, location and the need they described. This line tells the rep why the lead reached them.

**6. The next step they expect.** What the buyer was told would happen next, and by when.

## Build it with the tools you already have

You rarely need new software for this.

- **Capture source data on the form.** Hidden fields can record the landing page and campaign tags when the form is submitted, so the source line fills itself.
- **Add a note template to the CRM.** Six labeled fields, in the same order every time. Reps learn where to look, and gaps become visible.
- **Make one person responsible for lines 1 and 4.** Automation can fill in the source and the pages viewed. The buyer's own words and what they have been told usually need a person to copy them across.
- **Send the note with the lead, not after it.** A note that arrives an hour after the rep has called is a note nobody reads.

## How reps should use it

The note changes the first sentence of the call. Instead of "tell me about your business", the rep can say: "You asked whether we can set this up before March for a team of 15. Let me start there."

That opening does three things. It shows the buyer they were heard. It skips questions they have already answered. And it reaches the real conversation in the first minute rather than the tenth.

Reps should still confirm what the note says. People change their plans, and forms are filled in quickly. The note is a starting point, not a script.

## Test whether it works

Run it for a month and collect two kinds of feedback.

**From reps:** after each first call, one quick question in the CRM: "Did the note help? What was missing?" The missing items tell you which line to improve.

**From buyers:** in the follow-up email, one optional question: "Did you have to repeat anything you had already told us?" A "yes" points to the line that failed.

Review the answers at the end of the month and change one thing.

## A small note with a big effect

Buyers judge a company by how it treats them before they buy. Being asked to repeat yourself feels like being forgotten. A six-line note costs a few minutes per lead. For the buyer, it is the difference between continuing a conversation and filling in the same form twice.
