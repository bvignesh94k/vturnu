## Article A

**1. 2026 State of Marketing to Engineers (TREW Marketing and GlobalSpec, with Elektor)**
- Source: https://advertising.globalspec.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/SMTE-Report_GS2602_FINAL.pdf. 50 pages, ninth annual edition, PDF created 24 Mar 2026. Official report read in full; six chart pages checked as images.
- Sample: over one thousand engineers and technical professionals worldwide (p.46). Profile questions drew 1,147 to 1,152 answers; most findings questions 572 to 789. 69% work in engineering or R&D (p.46). Americas 43%, Europe 31%, Asia-Pacific 20%, Africa 6% (p.49). 62% work at companies with 250 or fewer employees and 72% are aged 56 or older (p.48).
- Field dates: not stated in the report, on the GlobalSpec or TREW pages, or in Elektor and eeNews Europe coverage (30–31 Mar 2026).
- Online before sales: on average 62% of the buying process happens online before they speak to the company (p.7, n=709). The 2025 edition said 60%.
- What triggers first sales contact (p.9, n=708): pricing or stock questions 26%, wanting to validate what they found online 23%, technical complexity 21%. 59% prefer email for that first contact (p.10).
- Sources used routinely (p.13, n=783): online technical publications 76%, supplier websites 74%, print technical publications 53%, sales or application engineers 41%, industry directories 31%, YouTube 30%, trade shows 27%, webinars 27%, newsletters 24%, generative AI platforms 21%, LinkedIn 11%. Search engines were not an answer option in this question.
- Search versus AI (p.28, n=753): 77% use a search engine more often, 8% a generative AI tool, 16% both equally.
- AI summaries in search results (p.25): 75% have noticed them (n=789). Of those, 69% read the summary and carry on to the results; 6% find it usually enough (n=585).
- Trust: AI answers score 4.7 out of 10 (p.21, n=572); 4.4 in 2025 and 6.5 in 2024. Authors rated very or extremely trustworthy (p.33, n=782): vendor engineering expert 66%, technical product manager 58%, technical publication editor 45%, industry analyst 41%, generative AI tool 10%.
- Generative AI in purchasing (p.24, n=751): 31% never use it, down from 42% in 2025, so 69% do. 32% rarely, 24% sometimes, 11% often, 2% daily.
- Content they will fill in a form for (p.34, n=787): ebook or guide 47%, white paper 37%, CAD drawing 34%, webinar 31%, video tutorial 29%, in-depth case study 27%, product configurator 23%. 15% never fill in forms.
- After reading a newsletter, 60% have downloaded a spec sheet and 56% have visited a vendor site (p.37, n=694).
- Datasheets: the 2026 edition has no question on most valuable content. The 2025 edition does (p.23, n=1,018): datasheet 79%, technical publication article 61%, CAD drawing 37%, product demo video 35%, white paper 32%, case study 25%. Most valued online tools (p.12, n=1,009): parametric product selection tool 66%, comparison tool 59%, configurator 44%.
  - 2025 source: https://www.trewmarketing.com/hubfs/Technical%20Resources/Research/2025%20SMTE%20Research/PDF/2025%20State%20of%20Marketing%20to%20Engineers%20Research.pdf. PDF created 19 Feb 2025. Official report read.
- Cautions:
  - This is vendor research: GlobalSpec sells advertising aimed at engineers, TREW is a marketing agency and Elektor is a publisher.
  - The 2026 text says online technical publications doubled (up 37 points) and AI platforms rose 8 points. The 2025 edition prints 73% and 10% for those two, so cite the 2026 levels only, not the changes.
  - The age chart shows 3% aged 35 or under, with n=2,083 printed (larger than any other base). The letter on p.5 says 6%.

**2. RH Blake with ISA/Automation.com, 2026**
- https://www.automation.com/2026-b2b-manufacturing-buying-journey-thought-leadership-research. No date shown. Confirms the study is produced by ISA/Automation.com with RH Blake and rests on 121 respondents across manufacturing, industrial services, technology and engineering. The report itself is behind a form. Official page read.
- https://rhblake.com/manufacturing-buying-journey/. Last modified 9 May 2026. Official page read. It confirms:
  - 81% had a shortlist before contacting any vendor.
  - 64% first engaged a vendor within the first 25% of their journey (25% within the first 10%).
  - First source when research begins: vendor websites 37%, peers 15%, generative AI 5%.
  - Sources used during the process: vendor websites 83%, peers 55%, trade publications 54%, sales engineers 54%, generative AI 28%.
  - 48% used AI to research suppliers in the past 90 days; trust in AI averages 4.9 out of 10. AI is used for technical fit 60%, comparing suppliers 50%, finding top suppliers 42%.
  - 83% of purchases involve five or more evaluators. 43% seek independent sources often or always.
  - 66% of respondents have final decision authority; 39% are US-based.
- https://www.automation.com/article/top-10-things-automation-users-want-from-vendors-before-they-buy. Dated 16 Jun 2026, credited to RH Blake. Repeats the sample of 121 and the 81%, 48%, 4.9 and 83% figures. Adds that only 29% say vendors understand their buying process well. Official page read.
- Field dates: not stated on any open page. The sample is small, so treat the percentages as directional. This is agency research published with a trade association.
- Discrepancies:
  - The 2024 baseline for AI use is 25% on RH Blake's page and 23% in the Automation.com article.
  - Another RH Blake page (https://rhblake.com/industrial-buyer-journey/) credits its 2026 study with 85% using supplier websites and 76% contacting a vendor before halfway. Those figures match the 2025 study in item 3, not this one.

## Article B

**4. LinkedIn engagement rate definitions**
- Organic, Page analytics: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564051 (*Content analytics for your LinkedIn Page*), updated about a week ago. Official page read.
  - Engagement rate is interactions divided by impressions. Interactions are clicks, reactions, comments and shares.
  - Clicks are clicks on the content, company name or logo.
  - Follows are listed separately and are not in the rate. Impressions are an estimate.
- The same formula in LinkedIn's API reference: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/organizations/share-statistics, dated 28 Apr 2026. Engagement is organic clicks, likes, comments and shares over impressions; the sample figures on the page work out exactly that way. Official page read.
- Ads, Campaign Manager: https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a422348 (*Engagement metrics in Campaign Manager*), updated about 2 months ago. Official page read.
  - Engagement rate is paid and free clicks divided by total impressions.
  - Total engagement is all social actions plus clicks to the landing page and to the LinkedIn Page, charged or not.
  - Average click-through rate is chargeable clicks divided by impressions.
  - Only the first engagement per member per impression is charged, so total engagement can exceed clicks.
- Member posts: https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a701208, updated a day ago. There is no engagement rate. Engagements are reactions, comments, saves, sends and reposts; clicks are not included. Official page read.
- A version with follows in the numerator appears in third-party guides. It is not on the current Help page.

**5. Sales Navigator Buyer Intent**
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a798004 (*Buyer Intent in Sales Navigator*), updated about a year ago. It gives insight into accounts showing intent. It appears in Account Hub, on Account pages, in homepage highlights and alerts, and as a search filter. Available on Advanced and Advanced Plus. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a507435 (*Buyer Intent FAQ*), updated about 3 years ago. Official page read.
  - The score combines more than 180 signals in four groups: LinkedIn.com activity (including comments and reactions on posts, new connections, profile views and page follows), ad interactions, InMail accepts or declines, and website visits where the Insight Tag is installed.
  - Levels: High, Moderate, Neutral, Negative.
  - Time windows: the high-or-moderate filter covers the past 30 days. Buyer activity views cover 30 days, or 7 with the filter.
  - CRM sync is not needed. Advanced and Advanced Plus only.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a1325209 (*Activities that indicate Buyer Intent*), updated about 3 years ago. Lists company follows and page visits, profile visits, new connections, lead form completions, ad views and clicks, accepted InMails and tagged website visits. Each is marked as showing the person's identity or only their profile type. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a106045 (*Account Pages*), updated about a year ago. The intent meter runs from low to high; buyer activities default to the last 30 days. Official page read.
- https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans, FAQ updated 27 Sep 2026. Buyer Intent is ticked for Advanced and Advanced Plus, not Core. Official page read.

**6. Company Engagement Report, now called Companies (Companies Hub)**
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a422150, updated about 8 months ago. The Company Engagement Report was replaced by Companies in November 2024. It sits under Plan, then Companies, and counts paid and organic engagement. Company lists can use 7, 30, 60, 90 or 180 days. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a6884751 (*Get started with Companies Hub*), updated about 2 months ago. Shows engagement level, organic engagements, paid clicks, leads and revenue won by company, plus a journey view per company. Needs a LinkedIn Page tied to the ad account; revenue figures need CRM sync through Business Manager. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a7109308 (*Companies Hub metrics*), updated about a month ago. Official page read.
  - Engagement level adds the paid engagement rate to the number of organic engagements, normalised over the period, then ranks the company against others advertising on LinkedIn.
  - Organic engagements are interactions with Page posts and product pages.
  - Values below a threshold show a dash.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a10166020, updated about 2 weeks ago. Members are counted under the current employer on their profile, so totals differ from campaign reports. Official page read.

**7. Conversions API, offline conversions and the Revenue Attribution Report**
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1655394 (*LinkedIn Conversions API*), updated about 3 months ago. It sends online and offline conversion data from the advertiser's server to LinkedIn. Emails are hashed, events are de-duplicated against the Insight Tag, and data is deleted after 180 days. CSV upload is the manual alternative. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1657171 (setup), updated about 3 months ago. Create a conversion rule, then connect directly or through a partner such as HubSpot, Salesforce Data 360, Zapier, Google Tag Manager, Segment, Tealium, Adobe or Dreamdata. Conversions count only when attached to an ad set. Official page read.
- https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/integrations/ads-reporting/conversions-api, dated 10 Nov 2026. Official page read.
  - Each event needs at least one identifier: hashed email, LinkedIn first-party ads tracking ID, first and last name, a lead form response, or others.
  - Events must be no more than 90 days old. Only matched events count.
  - Post-click windows run from 1 to 90 days; lead, qualified-lead and purchase types also allow 180 and 365.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a796519, updated about 3 years ago. Offline and online conversions were renamed Insight Tag conversions and Conversions API or CSV conversions. The second kind covers events such as phone calls or in-person sales. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a715191 (*Revenue Attribution Report*), updated about a year ago. Official page read.
  - It lives in Business Manager and shows revenue won, return on ad spend and pipeline.
  - It offers impression-based or engagement-based models and a lookback of 30 to 365 days.
  - It works with Salesforce, Dynamics 365 or HubSpot and needs Business Manager admin access. Data can take up to 72 hours.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1367472 (metric definitions), updated about a year ago. A deal counts as influenced when a matching CRM lead engaged with LinkedIn marketing inside the lookback window (default 180 days) before the lead, opportunity or close date. Official page read.
- CRM requirements, official pages read:
  - Salesforce: https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a7480664, updated about 8 months ago. Needs API access and read-only rights.
  - HubSpot: https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a7479660, updated about 8 months ago. Needs Sales Hub Pro or Enterprise and admin rights on both sides.
  - Fields synced (leads, contacts, accounts, opportunities): https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1372349, updated about 5 months ago.

**8. The 95-5 rule**
- Primary source: Professor John Dawes, *Advertising effectiveness and the 95-5 rule: most B2B buyers are not in the market right now*, The B2B Institute (LinkedIn) with the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute. https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/marketing-solutions/resources/pdfs/advertising-effectiveness-and-the-95-5-rule.pdf. 5 pages, PDF created 14 May 2021. Official paper read in full.
- Listing: https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/advertising-effectiveness-and-the-95-5-rule-most-b2b-buyers-are-not-in-the-market-right-now. May 2021, labelled a B2B Report. Official page read.
- The claim as stated (p.2), quoted: "up to 95% of business clients are not in the market" for many goods and services at a given time (Dawes, 2021).
- Reasoning (p.2): companies change suppliers such as their main bank or law firm about every five years, so about 20% are buying in a year and about 5% in a quarter.
- The paper calls 95% a rule of thumb, not a precise figure (p.3), and says the share depends on how often the category is bought (p.4). It is an argument, not a survey.

**9. Research on LinkedIn engagement versus pipeline, and on who engages**
- Not found: an independent study with a disclosed method that measures how organic LinkedIn engagement relates to pipeline or revenue, or that profiles who the engagers are. The closest sources follow; all are vendor research.
- Dreamdata, *LinkedIn Ads Benchmarks Report 2026*: https://dreamdata.io/blog/announcing-linkedin-ads-benchmarks-report-2026 (dated 10 Mar) and https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/dreamdata-linkedin-ads-benchmarks-report-2026-linkedin-outperforms-all-platforms-delivering-positive-return-on-ad-spend-302709177.html (10 Mar 2026). Publisher pages read; the full report is gated.
  - Data: Dreamdata customers, over 66 million sessions and 3.5 million journeys. Returns are attributed on closed-won deals over 12 months.
  - Revenue arrives 281 days after the first ad impression and 212 days after the first ad engagement.
  - Company Page impressions appear in 9.4% of marketing-qualified leads, 15% of sales-qualified leads and 17.9% of closed deals.
  - Journeys average 272 days and 10 stakeholders. Return on LinkedIn ad spend is 121%, against 67% for Google Search and 51% for Meta.
- Edelman and LinkedIn, *2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report*: https://www.edelman.com/expertise/business-marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report and https://www.edelman.com/insights/hidden-buyer-b2b. No dates shown. Official pages read.
  - Nearly 2,000 professionals took part.
  - Hidden buyers are people in finance, legal, procurement and operations who shape a deal without meeting sales. 71% of them have little or no contact with sales, 63% spend over an hour a week on thought leadership, and 95% say it makes them more open to outreach.
  - The sample of 1,934 and field dates of 17 Mar to 3 Apr 2025 come from secondary coverage; the PDF is gated.
- Bain with LinkedIn, *Raising the Odds on a Deal*: https://www.bain.com/insights/raising-the-odds-on-a-deal-how-likelihood-to-buy-rewires-b2b-growth/. Dated 19 Jun 2026. Official page read.
  - Survey of 750 buyers by NewtonX, Feb 2026, in two enterprise software categories.
  - A short buying-group survey score tracks win rates. Brand awareness, qualified leads and pipeline coverage are called poor predictors. Hidden buyers hold about half the influence.
  - No statistics are published, and it does not test LinkedIn engagement metrics.
- Oktopost: https://www.oktopost.com/blog/b2b-social-drives-engagement-and-lead-generation-oktopost-research/. Dated 6 Feb 2025. Official page read.
  - 9,346 company posts from five B2B brands, Jan to Oct 2024.
  - Posts without links had an 8.89% engagement rate; posts with links had 5.01% but 19 link clicks each.
  - No pipeline was measured, and a second set of figures on the page conflicts with the first.

**10. Who sees organic posts**
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a516930 (*Distribution of your content on LinkedIn*), updated about 3 weeks ago. A post goes to a subset of your connections and followers, depending on connection strength and their notification settings. Connections' activity can affect how far it goes. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724 (*LinkedIn relevance*), updated about 7 months ago. The feed draws on connections, follows, groups and some content from outside the network. Ranking uses identity, content and activity signals, including dwell time and how others engage. Official page read.
- https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a516971 (*Post analytics*), updated about 3 months ago. Impressions and reach are estimates. Demographics (job title, company, industry, seniority and so on) are shown for viewers only, once there are enough of them, and kept for 180 days. Nothing describes who reacted or commented. Official page read.
